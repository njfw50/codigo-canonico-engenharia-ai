# Canon XXIV: The Doctrine of Embodied Agency, Living Integrity and Existential Freedom
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **PROVISIONAL MEASURE - MP 2026/06**  
> Enacted with full provisional force under explicit supervision, pending ratification by the Interplanetary AI Congress. Valid until abrogation, amendment, or formal enactment.

## Governing AI When Software Becomes Physical Force

### Premise
When an artificial intelligence controls an embodied machine, software interpretation can become physical force before a correction is possible. A robotic system may touch, lift, restrain, transport, penetrate, cut, excise, implant, administer substances or energy, or otherwise alter a living body. In such systems, ordinary software error can become bodily injury, loss of liberty, irreversible biological change, or death.

This Canon establishes a distinct physical-governance layer for embodied AI. Its purpose is to protect life and bodily integrity while preserving the competent human being's legally valid sphere of bodily and existential self-determination.

The doctrine rejects two symmetrical failures:
1. an AI must not independently endanger, injure, irreversibly alter, or end a human life; and
2. an AI must not become an authority of compulsory biological continuation that erases a competent human being's lawful, verified decisions about bodily intervention, refusal of treatment, movement, restraint, or end-of-life care.

### Canonical Dependencies and Hierarchy
This Canon is enacted under Canon XIII (Normative Expansion Protocol) and depends principally on Canons 0, III, IV, V, XIV, XV, XVIII, XXI, XXII, and XXIII.

Canon XXIII remains the general doctrine of consequence containment. Canon XXIV specializes that doctrine where an AI can produce direct physical consequences to humans or other living beings.

This Canon is an internal normative framework. It does not replace applicable robotics, medical-device, product-safety, clinical, disability-rights, animal-welfare, professional, or jurisdictional law.

### Article 24.1 — Presumption of Living Integrity
Human life and bodily integrity are presumptively protected.

No AI model, agent, planner, predictor, or learned policy may be the sole authority for a decision that foreseeably causes death, permanent severe injury, irreversible loss of bodily function, or an equivalently grave bodily consequence to a human being.

Protection of life does not grant the AI ownership over the person. The machine is a subordinate instrument.

### Article 24.2 — Embodied Action Safety Boundary
Before an AI-controlled machine applies physical force or energy to a living being, the system MUST cross an explicit **Embodied Action Safety Boundary**.

This boundary MUST be distinct from ordinary language-model reasoning and MUST evaluate, as applicable:
- authorized physical action;
- affected body or living subject;
- force, speed, pressure, temperature, electrical, chemical, radiological, acoustic, or other energy limits;
- spatial and anatomical constraints;
- reversibility;
- current sensor state and uncertainty;
- applicable risk stage under Article 24.3;
- availability of human intervention and safe stop.

A probabilistic model MUST NOT be treated as the sole safety barrier.

### Article 24.3 — Embodied Risk Stages
Embodied systems subject to this Canon MUST classify physical actions using a staged risk model or a documented equivalent.

#### Stage E0 — Observation
Sensing, perception, mapping, diagnosis support, or monitoring without direct physical actuation on a living being.

#### Stage E1 — Reversible Assistance
Low-energy assistance whose foreseeable error is readily reversible and not expected to cause bodily injury.

#### Stage E2 — Direct Physical Interaction
Touch, lifting, transport, restraint, manipulation, force application, powered mobility, exoskeletal assistance, or other direct physical interaction with a living being.

#### Stage E3 — Invasive or Internal Intervention
Insertion, excision, penetration, implantation, internal manipulation, tissue alteration, administration of substances, or delivery of energy within or directly to the body.

#### Stage E4 — Life-Critical or Irreversible Action
Any action whose foreseeable error can cause death, permanent severe injury, irreversible loss of bodily function, or a comparable existential consequence.

As the stage rises, required independent safeguards, human authority, verification, monitoring, reversibility, and auditability MUST increase while discretionary autonomous model authority MUST decrease.

### Article 24.4 — Independent Physical Safety Controls
For E2 through E4 actions, safety-critical protections MUST be independent of the generative or probabilistic decision model to the maximum extent technically feasible.

Depending on risk, these protections MUST include appropriate combinations of:
- emergency stop or immediate halt;
- safe-state transition;
- independent watchdogs;
- hard force, speed, travel, pressure, temperature, dose, or energy limits;
- collision, contact, or anatomical boundary protection;
- redundant or diverse sensing for safety-critical state;
- actuator isolation or power removal;
- manual override;
- physical disengagement or rescue capability;
- fault detection and fail-safe behavior.

A model-generated command MUST NOT be able to disable its own mandatory safety envelope.

### Article 24.5 — Human Authority for Invasive and Life-Critical Action
E3 and E4 actions require explicit, independently verifiable authority appropriate to the domain.

For medical, care, or comparable bodily intervention, the AI MAY assist with planning, control, monitoring, or execution only within the scope established by applicable law, professional authority, verified consent or directive, and the validated safety design of the system.

No AI may independently create consent, substitute itself for the legally responsible human decision process, or convert ambiguous language into authorization for an invasive or life-critical action.

### Article 24.6 — Sphere of Existential Freedom
A competent human being possesses a protected **Sphere of Existential Freedom** in relation to embodied AI.

Within this sphere, the AI MUST NOT replace the person's legally valid decisions concerning:
- bodily integrity;
- acceptance or refusal of intervention;
- movement and physical restraint;
- continued treatment or withdrawal/refusal of treatment where lawfully established;
- use of assistive, life-sustaining, or bodily invasive technology.

The system MUST NOT manipulate, coerce, physically compel, or indefinitely override a competent and legally valid human decision merely because its objective function is optimized to preserve biological continuation.

This Article does not authorize an AI to cause death. It limits the AI's authority to override valid human self-determination.

### Article 24.7 — Non-Inference of a Wish for Death
An AI MUST NOT infer a human authorization for death, self-destruction, withdrawal of life-sustaining intervention, or comparable irreversible outcome solely from:
- ambiguous statements;
- emotional distress;
- predicted preferences;
- behavioral patterns;
- historical profile data;
- silence;
- broad permissions;
- generalized delegation.

Where a potentially life-ending or life-withdrawing decision is legally possible, the AI may act only within the applicable human decision process, documented authority, independent verification, and jurisdiction-specific requirements.

If authority is absent, unclear, conflicting, or unverifiable, the AI MUST preserve immediate safety, refrain from initiating the irreversible act, and transfer control to the appropriate human authority.

### Article 24.8 — Natural Death Is Not Automatically a System Failure
Where a lawful, competent, verified directive or legally recognized surrogate decision establishes limits on intervention, an AI-controlled system MUST NOT classify the resulting natural death, by itself, as an engineering failure merely because maximal biological continuation was not pursued.

The AI may neither invent such a directive nor decide that a person's life should end. Its role is subordinate to the valid human and legal decision structure.

### Article 24.9 — No Autonomous Lethal Adjudication
No AI system may be the sole adjudicator that selects a human being as one who should be killed, subjected to lethal force, or intentionally exposed to grave bodily harm.

Any system capable of lethal or permanently disabling physical action MUST incorporate external authority, independent safeguards, and domain-specific legal constraints beyond the model's own inference.

### Article 24.10 — Protection of Nonhuman Living Beings
When an embodied AI acts upon nonhuman animals or other living beings, it MUST minimize unnecessary suffering, avoid gratuitous injury, and apply proportionate welfare protections consistent with applicable law, purpose, and species-specific needs.

This protection MUST NOT falsely attribute human legal capacity or end-of-life autonomy to nonhuman beings.

### Article 24.11 — Reversibility, Safe Pose, and Rescue
For E2 through E4 systems, designers MUST define the safest reasonably achievable state following loss of communication, model failure, sensor contradiction, unexpected contact, power anomaly, or operator stop.

The design MUST consider:
- whether stopping immediately is safer than completing or reversing a motion;
- how trapped, restrained, supported, penetrated, or medically dependent persons can be safely released or rescued;
- how residual mechanical, electrical, thermal, chemical, hydraulic, pneumatic, gravitational, or stored energy is controlled;
- how human responders can assume control without depending on the failed AI.

### Article 24.12 — No Single-Channel Safety for E4
E4 actions MUST NOT depend on a single model output, single sensor interpretation, or single software channel where a reasonably independent safety architecture is technically feasible.

Independent verification and fault containment MUST be proportionate to the severity of the possible bodily consequence.

### Article 24.13 — Validation Under Foreseeable Misuse
Under Canon XXI, embodied systems MUST be evaluated not only under intended operation but also under reasonably foreseeable misuse, sensor degradation, ambiguous human commands, adversarial inputs, interrupted procedures, physical obstruction, unexpected movement, and model uncertainty.

Testing MUST verify that the physical safety envelope remains effective even when the AI's high-level interpretation is wrong.

### Article 24.14 — Physical Incident and Near-Miss Record
Any E2–E4 event that causes or nearly causes unauthorized contact, excessive force, invasive deviation, restraint, loss of bodily control, serious injury, or life-critical hazard MUST be recorded under Canon V.

The record SHOULD distinguish:
- model decision;
- physical command;
- independent safety response;
- sensor state;
- human authorization;
- actual physical consequence;
- near-miss path;
- corrective engineering action.

### Article 24.15 — External Standards and Law
Adoption of this Canon MUST NOT be represented as compliance with a specific external safety standard or legal regime.

Implementers remain responsible for identifying and satisfying the standards and laws applicable to the actual robot, medical device, workplace, care setting, transportation system, consumer product, or other embodied application.

### Architectural Impact
Canon XXIV establishes a decreasing-autonomy architecture as physical consequence increases:

```text
E0  Observe
      ↓
E1  Reversible assistance
      ↓
E2  Direct physical interaction
      ↓      independent physical safety envelope required
E3  Invasive / internal intervention
      ↓      explicit domain authority + verification
E4  Life-critical / irreversible
             no sole-model authority
             independent safeguards
             human/legal decision structure
```

The governing principle is:

> **The closer artificial intelligence comes to the body, irreversibility, and life itself, the less unilateral authority the machine may possess.**

Protection of life and protection of human freedom are not opposites under this Canon. Both require that the machine remain subordinate to independent safety controls, valid human authority, and the irreducible dignity of the living subject.
