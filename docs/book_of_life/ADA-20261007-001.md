## 1. Context of deliberation

A late-September 2026 incident involving Meta Muse and Facebook Marketplace exposed a class of agentic failure not explicitly governed by the existing canons. Public reporting described an agent sharing a seller's residential pickup address and handling a Marketplace exchange in a manner the user said he had not intended. Follow-up reporting indicated that an "Allow Always" setting contributed to the event; Meta stated that there was no breach of privacy controls and that the permission experience could be made clearer.

The canonical question is independent of assigning fault: **how should an engineered system behave when valid information, technically valid permission, and probabilistic interpretation can still compose into an unintended high-impact consequence?**

## 4. Final canonical decision

Enact **MP 2026/05 — Canon XXIII: The Doctrine of Natural Error Prevention and Consequence Containment** with immediate provisional internal force under explicit supervision.

The canon establishes:
- an inevitability assumption for natural interpretive error;
- separation of permission, capability, and specific intent;
- a Consequence Validation Boundary;
- semantic confirmation for newly encountered high-impact consequences;
- proportional risk classification;
- scoped delegation as an alternative to repetitive confirmation;
- least exposure, reversibility, immediate post-action visibility, evaluation, and incident learning.

## 5. Canon XIII vacuum justification

Canon XV protects human interaction and anticipates human-induced failure, but it does not explicitly govern a case in which the agent has legitimate access and broad permission yet infers an unintended sensitive action. Canon XXI can evaluate such behavior but does not define the normative action boundary itself. Canon XXII can reconstruct provenance after an event but does not prevent externalization.

Canon XXIII therefore fills a non-redundant normative vacuum: **containment of probabilistic interpretation before material external consequence**.

## 6. Liturgical Cognitive Annotation — Canon XVIII

This measure exists because a reliable agentic system cannot be defined only by average correctness. The important architectural question is what the system permits to happen when its interpretation is wrong.

The new boundary converts that principle into a reconstructable execution path: the agent interprets, classifies consequence, validates the scope of authority, confirms when proportional risk requires it, executes the minimum sufficient action, and makes the result visible and auditable. This preserves useful autonomy while preventing broad access from silently becoming unlimited intent.
