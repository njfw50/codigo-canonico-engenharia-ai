## 1. Context of deliberation

Following enactment of Canon XXIII on natural-error containment, the owner identified a remaining physical-domain vacuum: AI may increasingly control robots or machines that directly interact with living bodies, including systems capable of insertion, excision, physical assistance, restraint, movement, invasive treatment, energy delivery, or other bodily intervention.

The central question is not merely whether an AI can make a wrong prediction. It is whether an engineered system permits a probabilistic interpretation to become physical force against a living being without an independent safety structure.

The owner also introduced a second principle: preservation of life must not be converted into machine authority over the person's existence. A competent human being's legally valid bodily and end-of-life decisions require a protected sphere in which the AI remains subordinate.

## 2. Existing canonical coverage and vacuum

Consulted: Canons 0, III, IV, V, XIII, XIV, XV, XVIII, XXI, XXII, and XXIII.

Existing coverage:
- Canon XIV protects digital security and system integrity.

Vacuum:
None establishes the full physical architecture required when an AI can apply force or energy directly to a living body, nor the relationship between life preservation and a competent human being's legally valid bodily self-determination.

## 3. External engineering context

The following official standards were reviewed as contextual evidence that embodied robotics uses domain-specific safety treatment:
- ISO 10218-1:2025, *Robotics — Safety requirements — Part 1: Industrial robots*.
- ISO 10218-2:2025, *Robotics — Safety requirements — Part 2: Industrial robot applications and robot cells*.
- ISO 13482:2014, *Robots and robotic devices — Safety requirements for personal care robots*. As of October 2026, ISO lists a successor revision under development.
- IEC 80601-2-77:2019+AMD1:2023, *Medical electrical equipment — Particular requirements for the basic safety and essential performance of robotically assisted surgical equipment*.

These references inform the recognition of a distinct physical-safety domain. Canon XXIV does not reproduce those standards and does not claim conformance with them.

## 5. Final canonical decision

Enact **MP 2026/06 — Canon XXIV: The Doctrine of Embodied Agency, Living Integrity and Existential Freedom** with immediate provisional internal force under explicit supervision.

The doctrine establishes:
- Presumption of Living Integrity.
- Embodied Action Safety Boundary.
- Embodied Risk Stages E0–E4.
- Independent physical interlocks and safety envelopes.
- Reduced model autonomy as physical consequence and irreversibility increase.
- Explicit human/domain authority for invasive and life-critical action.
- Sphere of Existential Freedom.
- Prohibition on inferring a wish for death.
- Recognition that natural death under a valid human directive is not automatically an engineering failure.
- No sole-model lethal adjudication.
- Proportional welfare protection for nonhuman living beings.
- Safe-state, rescue, validation, incident-recording, and external-standards obligations.

## 6. Liturgical Cognitive Annotation — Canon XVIII

The distinction is architectural:

A software agent can often retry a failed operation. A machine touching a body may not have that luxury. Once force is applied, tissue cut, a substance administered, a person restrained, or a life-critical intervention altered, correction may be impossible.

Therefore, embodied AI must not place its highest-level probabilistic reasoning in the same safety channel as the mechanism that physically protects the living subject.

At the same time, a safety objective such as "keep the person alive" cannot be allowed to become an unlimited machine mandate that defeats a competent human being's lawful refusal of treatment or valid end-of-life directive. The AI protects life while remaining subordinate to human authority and law.
